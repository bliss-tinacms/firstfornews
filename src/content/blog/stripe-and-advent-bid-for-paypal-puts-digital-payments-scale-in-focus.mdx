A reported takeover proposal for PayPal is bringing together two different sides of the digital payments market: the consumer relationships built by a familiar checkout brand and the merchant infrastructure developed by Stripe. Stripe and private equity firm Advent International have offered $60.50 a share, valuing PayPal at more than $53 billion, according to Reuters. The approach remains a proposal, with no announced agreement between the companies.

The reported structure would give Stripe and Advent equal ownership of PayPal rather than divide the company. About $50 billion in bank financing has been committed to support the offer, sources told Reuters. The bid represents roughly a 28% premium to PayPal's previous closing price and follows an earlier approach in April.

The proposal has drawn attention because PayPal and Stripe serve overlapping markets while bringing different strengths to online transactions. PayPal has a recognizable consumer brand and owns Venmo, the peer-to-peer payment service. Stripe has expanded through merchant processing, e-commerce software and other financial services, with offices in South San Francisco and Dublin.

Payments Dive reported that analysts see potential in connecting those consumer and merchant relationships more closely. TD Cowen identified opportunities for a platform serving both sides of a transaction, including payments associated with AI-driven commerce. That is a possible strategic direction, rather than a product plan announced by either company.

Other analysts questioned whether the benefits would justify the purchase. William Blair analysts, cited by Payments Dive, argued that Stripe already has strong technology and an established position in e-commerce processing. PayPal could bring additional customers and a branded checkout button, but those assets would have to add enough value to support a large investment.

Advent's role adds another dimension to the proposal. The firm has invested in payment businesses, including Nuvei, and could provide capital and experience for a combined operation. Payments Dive reported that TD Cowen considered the pairing of an operating payments company and an experienced sector investor a credible approach to a transaction of this scale.

PayPal's position has changed substantially since its pandemic-era market peak. The Paypers reported that competing options such as Apple Pay and Google Pay have increased pressure on the company as consumers adopt alternative ways to pay. Its market value has fallen sharply from the approximately $360 billion level reached in 2021, making the current bid a very different proposition from a purchase at that peak.

Chief Executive Enrique Lores, who took over in March, has begun reorganizing the company. The Paypers described a structure covering checkout, Venmo, and payments and cryptocurrency, alongside efficiency efforts. Those changes give the board a separate question to consider: whether an acquisition would offer more value than continuing the turnaround independently.

The market reaction showed how strongly the possibility of a sale changed expectations. Reporting by 24/7 Wall St. placed PayPal's early July 15 share-price increase near 19%, while Reuters subsequently described gains of nearly 17%. Those figures reflect different points in the trading session and do not indicate that the transaction had been accepted or completed.

The proposed price also prompted debate about whether it was high enough. Investor Michael Burry argued that the offer undervalued PayPal, according to 24/7 Wall St., while other market observers raised similar concerns. Such views are assessments of potential value, not evidence that the buyers will increase their bid.

For now, the next development depends on the companies rather than the market reaction. PayPal, Stripe and Advent declined to comment on the reported approach, and the sources did not establish that an agreement would follow. A formal response, revised terms or a signed transaction would determine whether the bid becomes an actual consolidation of major digital payments businesses.
